<b>Key takeaways</b>

- The back side of an ID card does three jobs: **get a lost card returned, help in an emergency, and show the card is official and current**.
- Use six blocks, in this order of priority: **return text, organisation contact, validity date, emergency information (blood group, emergency phone), a short rule line, and the authorised signature**. Add a QR code or barcode only if someone actually scans it.
- On a standard CR80 card you have roughly **48 × 80 mm of safe area** on the back. At 7 pt that is about 11 lines of text in total, so plan for **6–8 short lines plus the signature**, not a paragraph of rules.
- Print the organisation's phone and address, never the holder's home address or personal mobile number (for children, print the guardian's number).
- Below you will find **ready-to-copy text in English and Bangla** for staff, student, factory, visitor, event and membership cards.

Most people design the front of an ID card carefully and then fill the back side of the ID card with whatever fits: a long rules paragraph, an address in tiny type, or nothing at all. The back is where a lost card finds its way home and where a first-aider looks in an emergency, so it deserves a plan.

This guide gives you the blocks that belong on the back, how much text actually fits, a to-scale layout, and a library of wording you can copy and adapt for every common card type, in English and Bangla.

## What is written on the back of an ID card?

An organisation's ID card (staff, student, member or visitor) usually carries six kinds of information on the back. They are listed here in the order you should drop them if space runs out, from most to least important.

| Block | What it says | Why it matters | Keep it to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Return text | "If found, please return to…" | A finder knows what to do with the card | 1–2 lines |
| 2. Organisation contact | Office address and phone | Where the card goes, who to call | 2 lines |
| 3. Validity | "Valid until 31 Dec 2027" or a session | Stops old cards being used | 1 line |
| 4. Emergency information | Blood group, emergency or guardian phone | First aid, accidents, a lost child | 1–2 lines |
| 5. Rule line | "Property of…, not transferable, must be shown on request" | Sets basic terms of use | 1–2 lines |
| 6. Authorised signature | Signature over name and title | Shows the card was issued by someone with authority | 10–12 mm tall block |
| Optional: QR code or barcode | Card or member number | Attendance, library, access, quick lookup | At least 15 × 15 mm |

The front identifies the holder (photo, name, role, ID number). The back answers the questions that come up after the first glance: whose card is this, is it still valid, and who do I call? For what goes on the front, see our guides to [employee ID card design](/blog/employee-id-card-design-what-to-include/) and [school ID card design](/blog/school-id-card-design-guide/).

## How much text fits on the back of a CR80 card?

A standard ID card is 85.6 × 54 mm, the ID-1 format of [ISO/IEC 7810](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO/IEC_7810), also called CR80. Keep 3 mm clear on every edge so cutting errors never trim text. That leaves about **79.6 × 48 mm** on a landscape back, or **48 × 79.6 mm** on a portrait back.

Here is the space budget, from our own calculation. One point is 0.353 mm, and we assume a normal sans-serif font at 1.25 line spacing.

| Text size | Line height | Lines in 48 mm (landscape) | Lines in 79.6 mm (portrait) | Characters per line, landscape (estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6.5 pt | 2.9 mm | 16 | 27 | about 70 |
| 7 pt | 3.1 mm | 15 | 25 | about 64 |
| 7.5 pt | 3.3 mm | 14 | 24 | about 60 |
| 8 pt | 3.5 mm | 13 | 22 | about 56 |

Now subtract the signature block (about 12 mm with its gap) and a little air between blocks. On a landscape back at 7 pt you realistically get **9–11 lines**, and if a 15 mm QR code sits on the right, each line shrinks to roughly 50 characters. Characters per line are an estimate: they vary by font, and Bangla script usually needs a size or so larger to stay readable.

So the rule is simple: **six to eight short lines plus a signature.** If your wording needs more, it belongs in the staff handbook or the school diary, not on the card.

A few layout rules that keep the back readable:

- **Use the same orientation as the front.** A portrait front with a landscape back makes people turn the card twice. If the card hangs on a lanyard, both sides should read upright when the card swings around.
- **Light background, dark text.** Pale tints or a faint logo watermark are fine; full-colour art behind 7 pt text is not.
- **One type size per block, two or three sizes in total.** Return text at 7.5–8 pt, contact and validity at 7 pt, the rule line at 6.5 pt. Never go below 6 pt.
- **Bold the label, not the whole line:** "**Valid until:** 31 Dec 2027" scans faster than a fully bold sentence.
- **Keep the signature real size.** A signature squeezed to 4 mm tall looks like a scribble; give it 8–10 mm of height over a printed name and title.

> **Tip:** The authorised signature looks cleanest when the paper behind it is removed. Sign on plain white paper with a dark pen, photograph it flat in good light and use a transparent PNG. CardCraft does this from a photo, so the signature sits on any background colour.

## Ready-to-copy text for the back of an ID card

Copy, then replace everything in square brackets. Each block is written to fit the space budget above. Use one return line and one rule line, not every variant.

### Return text ("if found")

> If found, please return to [Organisation Name], [short address], or call [office phone].

> This card belongs to [Organisation Name]. If found, please hand it in at the reception or call [office phone].

> Found this card? Please call [office phone] or drop it at [address]. Thank you.

### Rule line (terms of use)

> This card is the property of [Organisation Name]. It is not transferable and must be shown on request. Please report a lost card immediately.

> Misuse of this card may lead to disciplinary action. Return it when your employment ends.

These are common practice, not legal advice. If your organisation has a formal ID card policy, quote one line from it and keep the full policy in the handbook.

### Staff or office card

> Valid until: [31 Dec 2027] · Blood group: [B+]  
> Emergency: [HR or office phone]  
> If found, please return to [Company Name], [address] or call [office phone].

### Student card (school, madrasa, college)

> Guardian: [Name] · Phone: [number]  
> Blood group: [O+] · Session: [2026–2027]  
> If found, please return to [School Name], [address] or call [office phone].

For children, the guardian's number replaces any personal number, and the home address never appears. Our [school ID card design guide](/blog/school-id-card-design-guide/) covers the full child-safety list.

### Factory or worker card

> Section: [Sewing] · Line: [4] · Shift: [A]  
> Blood group: [A+] · Joining date: [01 Mar 2026]  
> Emergency: [factory office phone]  
> If found, please return to [Factory Name], [address].

In factories, guards often check the back too, so print the section and line in bold 8 pt.

### Visitor pass

> Visitor pass no. [017] · Date: [21 Nov 2026]  
> Must be worn visibly at all times and returned at the gate before leaving.  
> Visitors must be accompanied in restricted areas.

Reusable visitor passes should carry a pass number but no visitor name, so you can log the name at the desk and reuse the card.

### Event pass

> [Event Name] · [venue] · [dates]  
> Not transferable. Wear this pass at all times inside the venue.  
> Help desk: [phone]

### Membership card

> Member since: [2024] · Valid until: [31 Dec 2026]  
> Renew at [office or website]. This card remains the property of [Club Name].

## Bangla text for the back of an ID card

For organisations in Bangladesh, a Bangla back (or a Bangla return line under the English one) means any finder can read it. Use a clear Bangla font such as Hind Siliguri or Noto Sans Bengali, and keep Bangla text at 7.5 pt or larger.

| Purpose | Bangla text to copy |
|---|---|
| Return text | কার্ডটি কেউ পেয়ে থাকলে অনুগ্রহ করে [প্রতিষ্ঠানের নাম], [ঠিকানা]-এ ফেরত দিন অথবা [ফোন নম্বর]-এ যোগাযোগ করুন। |
| Property and transfer | এই কার্ডটি [প্রতিষ্ঠানের নাম]-এর সম্পত্তি এবং হস্তান্তরযোগ্য নয়। |
| Show on request | কর্তৃপক্ষ চাইলে কার্ডটি দেখাতে হবে। |
| Lost card | কার্ড হারিয়ে গেলে সঙ্গে সঙ্গে অফিসে জানান। |
| Validity | মেয়াদ: [৩১ ডিসেম্বর ২০২৭] পর্যন্ত |
| Blood group | রক্তের গ্রুপ: [বি+] |
| Guardian (students) | অভিভাবক: [নাম] · মোবাইল: [নম্বর] |
| Emergency | জরুরি যোগাযোগ: [অফিসের ফোন নম্বর] |
| Signature label | কর্তৃপক্ষের স্বাক্ষর / প্রধান শিক্ষকের স্বাক্ষর / অধ্যক্ষের স্বাক্ষর |

Pick one numbering style for the whole card. Bangla digits (২০২৭) read naturally in a Bangla line, but phone numbers are easier for everyone to dial in English digits, so many schools keep phone numbers in English digits even on a Bangla back.

## What not to put on the back of an ID card

The back is still public: anyone who finds the card can read it. Leave these off:

- **Home address** of the holder. A finder should reach the organisation, not the person's house.
- **Personal mobile number** of the holder, or of a teacher. Use the office or guardian number.
- **National ID, passport or birth certificate numbers.** They add nothing to an organisation card and are valuable to fraudsters.
- **Date of birth**, unless a rule truly requires it; for adults the joining date or validity is more useful.
- **Passwords, door codes or Wi-Fi details.** It happens more often than you would think.
- **A full rules paragraph** in 5 pt type. Nobody reads it, and it crowds out the lines that matter.

## Step by step: designing the back of your ID card

1. **Write the blocks first, design second.** Draft the return line, contact, validity, emergency line, rule line and signature label in a text file.
2. **Cut to 6–8 lines.** Delete any line that a finder, guard or first-aider would not use.
3. **Choose the orientation** to match the front, then place the zones: return band on top, details in the middle, signature at the bottom.
4. **Set the sizes:** 7.5–8 pt for return text, 7 pt for details, 6.5 pt for the rule line, and 7.5 pt or more for Bangla.
5. **Add the signature** as a transparent image 8–10 mm tall, over the signatory's printed name and title.
6. **Add a QR code or barcode only if it will be scanned**, at least 15 × 15 mm, holding the card or member number only.
7. **Test with the longest data** in your list: the longest guardian name, the longest section name.
8. **Print a proof double-sided** and hold it against a light to check the back lines up with the front. Our guide to [printing ID cards on A4 paper](/blog/how-to-print-id-cards-on-a4-paper/) explains how to mirror the back sheet for long-edge and short-edge flips.

## Back-side checklist before you print

- Return text is the first thing you read on the back.
- The organisation's address and phone are correct and answered during working hours.
- The validity date or session is present and correct for this batch.
- Blood group was confirmed by the holder or guardian, not guessed.
- No home address, personal mobile, national ID or date of birth is printed.
- All text sits at least 3 mm inside the card edge, and nothing is smaller than 6 pt.
- The signature is real size, transparent and above a printed name and title.
- A QR code, if used, is at least 15 × 15 mm and scans from a printed proof.
- Front and back have the same orientation and line up on the test print.
- Bangla text, if used, is checked by someone who reads Bangla fluently.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should be on the back of an ID card?

The back should carry "if found, please return" text, the organisation's address and phone, a validity date, emergency information such as blood group and an emergency or guardian phone, one short rule line and an authorised signature. Add a QR code or barcode only if someone will scan it.

### What do you write on the back of an ID card if found?

Keep it to one line that tells the finder exactly what to do: "If found, please return to [Organisation Name], [address], or call [office phone]." Use the office number rather than the holder's personal number, and add a Bangla version if most people in your area read Bangla.

### Is it necessary to print the back side of an ID card?

It is not required, but it is strongly recommended. Without a back, you must fit validity, return text and a signature on the front, which crowds the photo and name. Printing the back costs one more pass through the printer and gives you space for the information that gets lost cards returned.

### What size should the text be on the back of an ID card?

On a standard CR80 card, use 7.5–8 pt for return text, 7 pt for details and 6.5 pt for the rule line, and never go below 6 pt. Bangla text needs about 7.5 pt or more to stay readable when printed.

### Should the signature be on the front or back of an ID card?

Either works, but the back is more common because it leaves the front for the photo and name. Put the signature where it will not be covered by a holder or clip, give it 8–10 mm of height and print the signatory's name and title under it.

### Can I put terms and conditions on the back of an ID card?

Yes, but only one or two lines, such as "property of the organisation, not transferable, must be shown on request". Full terms belong in a handbook or policy document; on a card they end up in unreadable 5 pt type.
