School ID Card Design: Student Fields, Layout and Renewal

School ID card design for schools and madrasas: exact student fields for front and back, what to keep off for child safety, sizes and a yearly renewal plan.

By Md. Ruhul Amin19 November 202613 min read

Key takeaways

  • The front of a school ID card needs school logo and name, student photo, full name, class, section, roll or student ID, and the session year. The session year matters most, because it tells a guard at a glance whether the card is current.
  • The back carries guardian name and phone, blood group, the school address and phone, "if found" text and the head teacher's or principal's signature.
  • For children, never print the home address, date of birth or the student's own mobile number. A lost card should lead a stranger back to the school, not to the child's home.
  • Use portrait CR80 cards (54 × 85.6 mm) on breakaway lanyards for most schools. Make the student's name 11–13 pt and the class and section 9–10 pt, so a teacher can read it from a short distance.
  • Plan the yearly renewal before you design: one layout, a session colour that changes each year, and a student list kept as a spreadsheet you can reuse.

School ID card design looks simple until you have to make 800 cards in the first two weeks of the school year. Fields get forgotten, photos come in different sizes, and next January the whole job starts again.

This guide gives you a field-by-field plan for the front and back of a student ID card, what to leave off for child safety, exact sizes for a standard CR80 card, layouts for primary schools, high schools, colleges and madrasas, and a renewal workflow you can repeat every year.

What should be on a school ID card?

A student card is checked by a gatekeeper, a class teacher, an exam invigilator, a librarian or a bus attendant. Each needs a few things fast. Put those on the front and everything else on the back.

Field Side Required? Who uses it Practical note
School logo and name Front Yes Everyone Logo 8–12 mm tall; short name if the full name is long
Student photo Front Yes Gate, exams About 26 × 32 mm on a portrait card, plain light background
Full name Front Yes Teachers, exams Same spelling as the admission register
Class and section Front Yes Teachers, gate "Class 7 · Section B" reads faster than "VII-B" for younger children
Roll or student ID number Front Yes Office, library, exams Use a permanent student ID if you have one; roll numbers change each year
Session or valid year Front Yes Gate "Session 2027" in a colour band; change the colour every year
Guardian name and phone Back Yes Office, anyone who finds a child One number that is answered during school hours
Blood group Back Common in South Asia First aid Print only what the guardian has confirmed
School address and phone Back Yes Anyone who finds the card Office number, not a teacher's personal number
"If found" text Back Recommended Finder One or two lines
Head teacher or principal signature Back Recommended Shows the card is official Transparent signature over printed name and title
Bus route or house Back or front Optional Transport, sports Only if staff actually check it
QR code or barcode Back Optional Attendance, library Encode the student ID only, at least 15 × 15 mm

Two fields cause most of the trouble. Roll number changes when students are re-ranked or move class, so a card that shows only the roll number goes stale. If your school keeps a permanent student ID (for example year of admission plus a serial, such as 2027-0415), print that as the main number and add the roll number beside it. Session year is the field many templates forget, yet it is the simplest way to stop last year's cards being used.

A few layout rules that work well with children's cards:

  • Name centred under the photo. Teachers and guards look at the face, then straight down.
  • Class and section on one line. Younger children's cards are checked by bus and gate staff who need class more than roll number.
  • Session in the bottom band. A full-width coloured band with "SESSION 2027" in large text is visible from across a gate. Pick a new colour each year so old cards stand out.
  • The 3 mm safe zone stays empty. Keep text and the photo at least 3 mm from every edge so cutting errors do not trim them. Our ID card size chart has the pixel sizes for this at 300 DPI.

What not to print on a student ID card

Children's cards need stricter privacy than staff cards. A child might lose a card on the way home, and the finder could be anyone. The test for every field: if a stranger read this, would it help them find, follow or impersonate the child?

Do not print Why What to do instead
Home address Tells a stranger where the child lives Print the school's address for returns
Date of birth With name and photo, it helps someone pose as a relative or official Keep it in the admission register
Student's own mobile number Invites contact from strangers Print the guardian's number or the school office
Birth registration or national ID number Can be misused; it is not needed at the gate Link records through the student ID
Father's occupation, income or religion Sensitive, and irrelevant to identification Leave it off
Medical conditions beyond blood group Health data needs consent Keep allergy and medication notes with the class teacher and nurse
Exam results or merit position Can cause embarrassment or bullying Not an ID matter

Some schools print the guardian's phone on the front so that anyone can call quickly if a young child is lost. That is reasonable for nursery and early primary, but put it on the back for older students, who are more likely to have the card seen by people around them.

Tip: If you add a QR code for attendance or the library, encode only the student ID number. Anyone with a phone camera can read a QR code, so it should never contain the guardian's details or a full record.

Sizes, fonts and photo specs for school cards

The standard card is CR80: 85.6 × 54 mm (3.370 × 2.125 in), used in portrait as 54 × 85.6 mm. At 300 DPI that is 638 × 1011 pixels. These sizes have worked well for student cards in our templates:

Element Portrait CR80 size Notes
Student name 11–13 pt, bold Two lines allowed for long names
Class, section, roll 9–10 pt Bigger than on staff cards; children's cards are read by many people
Student ID number 8–9 pt, bold Same number as the office register
Session band text 11–14 pt, bold Full width, high contrast
Back-side text 6.5–8 pt Guardian phone 8 pt bold
Photo 26 × 32 mm Head fills about 60–70% of the photo height
QR code 15–18 mm square Keep a 2 mm quiet margin around it
Lanyard slot Centred, about 3 mm from the top edge Keep the header text clear of the punch

For Bangla names, use a proper Unicode Bangla font such as Hind Siliguri or Noto Sans Bengali so conjuncts render correctly, and allow a little more line height than for English. If you print names in both scripts, keep the English spelling identical to the admission register and, for board exam classes, to the board registration.

Breakaway lanyards and holders for children

For a school, how the card is worn matters as much as what is printed on it. A normal lanyard is a closed loop around the neck. If it catches on playground equipment, a bus door or another child's hand, it cannot open. A breakaway lanyard has a plastic safety clasp, usually at the back of the neck, that pulls apart under force and can be snapped back together afterwards.

  • Nursery and primary: breakaway lanyard, or a clip on the shirt pocket or bag strap. Many schools keep younger children's cards on the school bag rather than the neck.
  • High school and college: breakaway lanyard in the school colour; a rigid or soft PVC holder protects a paper card through the year.
  • Labs, workshops and sports: take lanyards off or tuck the card in; any loose cord near machines or in contact sports is a risk.

A portrait card needs a portrait holder and a lanyard slot at the top centre. If you laminate the cards, punch the slot after laminating, not through the printed area.

The same core layout works everywhere, with a few changes in the fields:

Madrasa cards need a little care with names and class labels. Alia madrasas use stages such as Ebtedayee, Dakhil and Alim; Qawmi madrasas often use the jamat name. Print the label the madrasa actually uses on its register, and if you add an Arabic name, test that the font joins letters correctly before printing a full batch. Photo backgrounds should be plain and light, the same as for any other card; follow the madrasa's own dress guidance for the photo.

The yearly renewal workflow

Most schools in Bangladesh run the academic year from January to December, so the card rush comes in the first weeks of the year, along with new admissions and class changes. A repeatable system saves days of work:

  1. Keep one master spreadsheet. Columns: student ID, name (English), name (Bangla), class, section, roll, session, guardian name, guardian phone, blood group, photo file name. Re-use it every year instead of starting over.
  2. Promote everyone in the sheet. Update class, section and roll after results, add new admissions and remove students who have left.
  3. Hold a photo day per class. One teacher calls names from the list, one person takes photos against the same plain wall, and each photo is named with the student ID. Retake only those who changed a lot or are new.
  4. Change the session colour and year in the template. Everything else stays the same, so cards stay consistent from year to year.
  5. Print a proof of one class first. Check spelling with the class teacher, measure a card at 100% scale (85.6 × 54 mm) and check that the back lines up with the front.
  6. Print the rest on A4 sheets. Nine portrait CR80 cards fit on one A4 sheet (3 × 3), so a class of 45 needs five sheets per side; our guide to printing ID cards on A4 paper covers the margins, cut marks and duplex alignment.
  7. Hand out by class with a signed list. Keep a few blank holders for lost cards and record replacements.

Checklist before you print the whole school

  1. The front shows logo and school name, photo, full name, class, section, roll or student ID, and session year.
  2. The student's name is the largest text (11–13 pt) and the longest name in the list fits without touching the photo or edge.
  3. Session year is printed in a band whose colour is different from last year's.
  4. No home address, date of birth, student mobile number or ID-document number appears anywhere.
  5. The back has guardian name and phone, school address and phone, "if found" text and the head's signature.
  6. Blood groups have been confirmed by guardians, not guessed.
  7. Photos are sharp, on the same plain background, and roughly the same head size.
  8. Student ID numbers match the office register, with no duplicates.
  9. Bangla (and Arabic, if used) text renders correctly in a printed proof.
  10. A test card measures 85.6 × 54 mm, the lanyard slot is clear of text, and breakaway lanyards are ordered for younger classes.

Frequently asked questions

What information should be on a student ID card?

The front should show the school logo and name, the student's photo, full name, class, section, roll or student ID number and the session year. The back should carry the guardian's name and phone, blood group, the school's address and phone, return text and the head teacher's signature. Leave off the home address and date of birth.

What is the size of a school ID card?

Most school ID cards use the CR80 size, 85.6 × 54 mm (3.370 × 2.125 in), the same as a bank card, usually in portrait orientation for lanyards. At 300 DPI the design is 638 × 1011 pixels in portrait. Some nursery schools use larger badge sizes so the name is easier to read.

Should a school ID card be portrait or landscape?

Portrait suits most schools because the card hangs straight on a lanyard with the photo at the top, where gate staff look first. Landscape is better if students carry the card in a wallet or a clip holder, or if the school's name is very long. Use one orientation for all students so cards are easy to check.

Should the guardian's phone number be on a student ID card?

Yes, for school-age children it is the most useful field on the back, because whoever finds a lost child or card can call someone who will answer. Print one number that is reachable during school hours, or the school office number for older students. Do not print the child's own mobile number.

How often should student ID cards be renewed?

Most schools issue new cards every academic year, because class, section and roll change after promotion. Showing the session year in a coloured band makes expired cards easy to spot. Colleges sometimes issue one card for a two-year HSC cycle and change only a sticker or the back.

Why do children need breakaway lanyards?

A normal lanyard is a closed loop that cannot open if it catches on something, which can choke or drag a child. A breakaway lanyard has a safety clasp that pulls apart under force and clips back together. For nursery and primary pupils, a clip on the bag or uniform is another safe option.
